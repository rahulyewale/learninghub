1. What is a Polynomial?

A polynomial in one variable is an algebraic expression of the form anxn + an-1xn-1 + ... + a1x + a0, where the powers of x are whole numbers and coefficients are real numbers.

Important: Exponents must be non-negative integers. No variable in denominator or inside root.

3. Degree of a Polynomial

The degree is the highest power of the variable in a polynomial.

Degree of 7x⁴ - 3x² + x - 9 = 4

Polynomial operations

  • Addition and subtraction of polynomials
  • Multiplication by monomials / polynomials
  • Evaluation of a polynomial at a value
NCERT focus: Learn to identify terms, coefficients and degree correctly.

4. Zeroes of a Polynomial

The zero of a polynomial p(x) is the value of x for which p(x) = 0.

For example, for p(x) = x - 3, the zero is x = 3.

Graph idea

The zeroes of a polynomial are the x-coordinates where its graph intersects the x-axis.

Exam tip: A polynomial may have one, two, or more zeroes depending on its degree.

5. Sample Questions with Answers

Q1. Is x + 1 a polynomial? Why? 1 Mark
Answer: Yes. The variable has a non-negative integer power and the coefficients are real.
Q2. Find the degree of 4x³ - 2x + 7. 1 Mark
Answer: 3
Q3. Classify x² + 3x + 1. 1 Mark
Answer: It is a trinomial and a quadratic polynomial.
Q4. Find a zero of x - 5. 2 Marks
Answer: Set x - 5 = 0, so x = 5 is the zero.
Q5. Differentiate between monomial and binomial. 2 Marks
Answer: A monomial has one term, while a binomial has two terms.
Q6. Why is 1/x not a polynomial? 2 Marks
Answer: Because x is in the denominator, which gives a negative power of x.

6. Quick Revision Tips

  • Only whole-number powers are allowed
  • Count terms to classify monomial/binomial/trinomial
  • Degree is the highest power
  • Zeroes satisfy p(x) = 0
  • Do not confuse coefficient with degree
