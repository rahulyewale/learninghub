1. Introduction to Digital Communication

Digital Communication is the process of exchanging information using digital devices and the internet — including emails, social media, messaging apps, and video conferencing.

Traditional vs Digital Communication

TraditionalDigital
Postal letters (slow)Email (instant)
Landline telephoneVoIP / Video call
Newspaper / Notice boardSocial media / website
Physical meetings onlyOnline video conferencing

Advantages of Digital Communication

  • Very fast – messages reach in seconds
  • Inexpensive – low cost compared to postal mail
  • Global reach – communicate across the world
  • Multimedia support – text, images, audio, video
  • Stored records – messages can be saved and retrieved
  • Accessible 24/7

Disadvantages

  • Requires internet connectivity
  • Risk of hacking and privacy breach
  • Spread of misinformation (fake news)
  • Reduced face-to-face interaction
  • Digital addiction / screen time issues

3. Electronic Mail (Email)

Email (Electronic Mail) is a method of exchanging digital messages over the internet. It is fast, reliable, and supports attachments.

Parts of an Email Address

username @ domain . extension
Example: devanshi@gmail.com
  • devanshi = username (unique to the user)
  • @ = 'at' symbol separating user and domain
  • gmail = email service provider / domain
  • .com = domain extension (type of organisation)

Structure of an Email

From: sender@example.com
To: receiver@example.com
CC: carbon copy – other recipients who receive a copy
BCC: blind carbon copy – hidden recipients
Subject: Brief topic of the email
Attachment: Files attached (documents, images, PDFs)
Body: The main content / message
Signature: Sender's name, designation, contact

To vs CC vs BCC

FieldFull FormUse
To—Primary recipient of the email
CCCarbon CopyOthers who should be informed; all recipients can see CC addresses
BCCBlind Carbon CopyRecipients are hidden from others; useful for privacy/bulk mailing

How to Compose a Formal Email

To: principal@school.edu
Subject: Request for Leave

Dear Sir/Madam,

I am writing to request a leave of two days (12–13 June 2026) due to a family function. I will complete all missed assignments on my return.

Thank you for your consideration.

Yours sincerely,
Devanshi Sharma
Class 9 – Section A

Popular Email Services

  • Gmail (Google)
  • Outlook / Hotmail (Microsoft)
  • Yahoo Mail
  • Rediff Mail (India)

Email Attachment Tips

  • Keep attachment size small (compress if needed)
  • Mention the attachment in the email body
  • Use common formats: PDF, DOCX, JPG, PNG, ZIP
  • Do NOT open attachments from unknown senders

4. Netiquette – Online Etiquette

Netiquette (Network + Etiquette) refers to the accepted rules of behaviour for communication on the internet.

Rules of Netiquette

#RuleDescription
1Be RespectfulTreat others online as you would in person
2No SHOUTINGAVOID TYPING IN ALL CAPS — it implies shouting
3Be Clear & ConciseWrite clear, short and meaningful messages
4Respect PrivacyDo not share others' personal information without permission
5No CyberbullyingDo not send hurtful or threatening messages
6Proofread before sendingCheck for errors and tone before sending an email
7Reply PromptlyRespond to emails/messages within a reasonable time
8Use Correct Subject LineEmail subject should clearly describe the content
9Avoid SpamDon't send unnecessary bulk or repetitive messages
10Respect Intellectual PropertyGive credit when sharing others' work

Email Etiquette Specifically

  • Always fill in a meaningful Subject line
  • Start with a proper greeting (Dear Sir/Hello Rahul)
  • Keep the language formal in official emails
  • End with a closing (Regards / Yours sincerely)
  • Do not forward chain mails or hoaxes
  • Reply to all only when everyone needs to know
Cyberbullying: Using the internet to harass, threaten, or harm someone. It is ILLEGAL and should be reported immediately.

5. Social Media & Instant Messaging

Social Media are online platforms that allow users to create, share content, and interact with others.

Popular Social Media Platforms

Instant Messaging (IM)

Instant Messaging is the exchange of text messages in real time over the internet.

  • Examples: WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal, Google Chat
  • Supports text, voice, video, images, documents
  • Group chats allow multiple people to communicate together

Advantages of Social Media

  • Connects people globally
  • Fast spread of news and information
  • Business promotion and marketing
  • Educational content (YouTube, blogs)

Disadvantages / Risks

  • Fake news and misinformation
  • Privacy threats (data misuse)
  • Cyberbullying and online harassment
  • Addiction and reduced productivity
  • Phishing and fraud through fake accounts

6. Digital Communication Tools

A. Video Conferencing

Video Conferencing is a live audio-video call between two or more participants over the internet.

ToolProviderUse
ZoomZoom Video CommunicationsMeetings, webinars, online classes
Google MeetGoogleOnline meetings, school/office
Microsoft TeamsMicrosoftEnterprise collaboration
SkypeMicrosoftPersonal and business calls

B. Blogs

A Blog (web log) is an informational website written in a conversational style, typically maintained by an individual or small group.

  • Content is displayed in reverse chronological order
  • Allows comments and interaction with readers
  • Examples: WordPress, Blogger, Medium

C. Wikis

A Wiki is a website that allows any user to add, edit, or delete content collaboratively.

  • Best example: Wikipedia
  • Used in schools and businesses for collaborative documentation

D. Online Forums & Discussion Boards

Online spaces where users can post questions, answers and participate in discussions. Example: Quora, Reddit, Stack Overflow.

E. VoIP (Voice Over Internet Protocol)

VoIP allows making voice calls using the internet instead of a traditional phone line.

  • Examples: WhatsApp calls, Google Voice, Skype
  • Much cheaper than traditional STD/ISD calls

7. Online Safety & Digital Footprint

Digital Footprint

A Digital Footprint is the trail of data you leave behind while using the internet — including posts, searches, emails, photos, and purchases.

TypeDescriptionExample
Active FootprintData you intentionally shareSocial media posts, emails sent
Passive FootprintData collected without your direct inputBrowser cookies, IP address tracking
Remember: Once posted online, information is hard to remove. Think before you post!

Cyber Safety Rules

  • Use strong passwords (mix of letters, numbers, symbols)
  • Never share passwords with anyone
  • Do not click on suspicious links or attachments
  • Log out from accounts on shared devices
  • Enable two-factor authentication (2FA)
  • Do not share personal info (address, phone, school name) with strangers online
  • Report cyberbullying to a trusted adult or authority

Common Online Threats

ThreatDescription
PhishingFake emails/websites tricking users into giving personal/financial info
MalwareMalicious software (virus, worm, spyware) that damages devices
SpamUnwanted bulk email, often for advertising or fraud
HackingUnauthorised access to someone's account or device
CyberbullyingHarassment, threats, or humiliation using digital means
Identity TheftStealing someone's personal information to impersonate them

Strong Password Tips

  • At least 8 characters long
  • Mix of UPPERCASE, lowercase, numbers (0-9), symbols (!@#$)
  • Do not use your name, birth date, or easy words
  • Use different passwords for different accounts
  • Change passwords regularly

Section B – Short Answer Questions 2 marks each

Q6. What is an email? Name any three parts of an email message. 2 Marks
Model Answer:

Email (Electronic Mail) is a method of exchanging digital messages over the internet. It can contain text, images, and file attachments.

Three parts of an email:

  1. Subject: Brief description of the email topic
  2. Body: The main content or message of the email
  3. Attachment: Files (documents, images, PDFs) sent along with the email

Model Answer:
CC (Carbon Copy)BCC (Blind Carbon Copy)

Q8. What is netiquette? State any four rules of netiquette. 2 Marks
Model Answer:

Netiquette = Network + Etiquette. It refers to the code of polite behaviour accepted for online communication.

Four Rules:

  1. Be respectful and polite in all online communication
  2. Avoid typing in ALL CAPS (it implies shouting)
  3. Do not share others' personal information without permission
  4. Do not send spam or chain messages
Q9. What is a digital footprint? Give one example each of active and passive digital footprint. 2 Marks
Model Answer:

A digital footprint is the trail of data/information left behind by a user on the internet.

  • Active footprint: Data shared intentionally — e.g., posting a photo on Instagram
  • Passive footprint: Data collected without direct knowledge — e.g., website cookies tracking your browsing

Q13. Explain the advantages and disadvantages of social media. Also describe any five rules of netiquette a student should follow online. 5 Marks
Model Answer:

Social Media are internet-based platforms that allow users to create, share, and interact with content.

Advantages:

  1. Connects people globally regardless of location
  2. Instant spread of news and awareness
  3. Platform for education (YouTube tutorials, study groups)
  4. Business promotion and digital marketing
  5. Helps maintain relationships with family and friends

Disadvantages:

  1. Spread of fake news and misinformation
  2. Privacy issues and data misuse by platforms
  3. Cyberbullying and online harassment
  4. Addiction causing reduced focus on studies
  5. Exposure to inappropriate content

Five Netiquette Rules for Students:

  1. Be respectful: Treat others online as you would in person — no insults or hate speech
  2. No CAPS: Typing in ALL CAPITALS is considered shouting — avoid it
  3. Protect privacy: Do not share others' personal details without consent
  4. Think before posting: Online posts are permanent; once posted they are hard to remove
  5. Avoid cyberbullying: Do not send hurtful, threatening, or embarrassing messages/images
