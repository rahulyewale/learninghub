1. What is Communication?

Communication is the process of exchanging information, ideas, thoughts, feelings or knowledge between two or more people using a common system of symbols, signs, or behaviour.

Importance of Communication

  • Builds personal and professional relationships
  • Helps in sharing and spreading information
  • Improves teamwork and collaboration
  • Reduces misunderstandings and conflicts
  • Helps express needs, ideas and emotions
  • Supports learning and development

Elements of Communication

ElementDescription
SenderPerson who initiates or sends the message
MessageThe information/idea to be communicated
EncodingConverting idea into a communicable form (words, signals)
Channel / MediumThe path used to send the message (speech, email, letter)
ReceiverPerson who receives the message
DecodingInterpreting and understanding the received message
FeedbackResponse from the receiver back to the sender
NoiseAny disturbance/interference that distorts the message
Remember: Communication is a two-way process. Without feedback it remains one-way (like a broadcast).

2. Types / Methods of Communication

TypeMeaningExamples
VerbalUsing words (spoken or written)Speech, phone, letter, email
Non-VerbalWithout words; body languageGestures, expressions, eye contact
VisualUsing images, symbols, graphicsCharts, diagrams, posters, signs

A. Verbal Communication

i. Oral / Spoken Communication

  • Face-to-face conversation
  • Telephone / Video call
  • Meetings and conferences
  • Speeches and presentations

Advantages: Immediate feedback, personal touch, quick clarification.

Disadvantages: No permanent record, may be misunderstood, affected by noise.

ii. Written Communication

  • Letters, notices, reports
  • Emails, SMS, WhatsApp messages
  • Books, newspapers

Advantages: Permanent record, precise, can be referred later.

Disadvantages: Time-consuming, no immediate feedback, requires literacy.

B. Non-Verbal Communication

FormExample
Facial ExpressionsSmiling (happiness), frowning (displeasure)
Eye ContactDirect eye contact shows confidence
Posture / Body LanguageStanding straight shows attentiveness
Hand GesturesWaving hello / thumbs up
Tone of VoiceLoud vs soft; happy vs angry
Personal SpaceProximity in conversation
Fact: Studies show that over 70% of communication is non-verbal (body language + tone).

C. Visual Communication

  • Maps, graphs, charts
  • Traffic signs and signals
  • Infographics and posters
  • Emojis and icons (digital)

3. The Communication Cycle (Process)

Sender ➜ Idea/Thought ➜ Encoding ➜ Channel ➜ Receiver ➜ Decoding ➜ Feedback
↩ Feedback loops back to the Sender completing the cycle

Explanation of Each Step

  1. Sender – Has an idea or information to share.
  2. Idea – The content or thought that needs to be communicated.
  3. Encoding – The sender converts the idea into words, symbols or signals.
  4. Channel – The medium through which the message travels (voice, email, paper).
  5. Receiver – The person who receives the message.
  6. Decoding – The receiver interprets and understands the message.
  7. Feedback – The receiver responds, completing the cycle.
Noise can enter at any stage – it is anything that interrupts or distorts the message (loud environment, poor signal, language barrier).

4. Barriers to Communication

Barriers are obstacles that prevent effective communication.

Type of BarrierMeaningExample
Physical / EnvironmentalPhysical distance or noiseLoud room, poor internet
LanguageDifferent language or vocabularyUsing jargon or technical terms
PsychologicalMental state or attitudeFear, stress, prejudice
EmotionalStrong feelings affecting receptionAnger, grief
CulturalDifferent customs / valuesDifferent meanings of gestures
PerceptualDifferent understanding of the same messageVisual perception differences
TechnologicalDevice or network failureNo signal, power cut

Ways to Overcome Barriers

  • Use simple, clear language
  • Choose the right channel
  • Listen actively and ask questions
  • Be aware of cultural differences
  • Seek feedback to confirm understanding

5. Feedback

Feedback is the response or reaction of the receiver after receiving and decoding the message.

TypeDescriptionExample
Positive FeedbackConfirms message was understood correctly"Yes, I understand."
Negative FeedbackIndicates misunderstanding or disagreement"I'm not sure what you mean."
Verbal FeedbackSpoken or written responseReplying to an email
Non-Verbal FeedbackBody language, expressionNodding, frowning
Importance of Feedback: It helps the sender know if the message was received and understood correctly. It makes communication a two-way process.

6. Listening Skills

Active Listening means fully concentrating on the speaker, understanding the message, and responding appropriately.

Types of Listening

TypeDescription
Active ListeningFully focused, engaged, asks questions
Passive ListeningHearing without full attention or response
Empathetic ListeningListening with feelings/emotions
Critical ListeningEvaluating and analysing the content

Tips for Good Listening

  • Maintain eye contact with the speaker
  • Do not interrupt; wait for your turn
  • Avoid distractions (phone, noise)
  • Take brief notes if needed
  • Ask clarifying questions
  • Summarise what you heard
Remember: Listening ≠ Hearing. Hearing is passive; Listening is active and conscious.

7. Writing & Reading Skills

Effective Writing

  • Use simple and clear sentences
  • Organise ideas logically
  • Check spelling and grammar
  • Use appropriate format (letter, email, report)
  • Proofread before sending

Effective Reading

  • Skimming: Reading quickly to get the main idea
  • Scanning: Looking for specific information
  • Intensive Reading: Reading carefully for details
  • Extensive Reading: Reading for overall understanding/pleasure

Format of a Formal Letter

  1. Sender's Address
  2. Date
  3. Receiver's Address
  4. Subject
  5. Salutation (Dear Sir/Madam)
  6. Body (Introduction, Content, Conclusion)
  7. Complimentary close (Yours sincerely/faithfully)
  8. Sender's Signature and Name

Q7. What is feedback? Why is it important in communication? 2 Marks
Model Answer:

Feedback is the response or reaction given by the receiver to the sender after receiving and understanding the message.

Importance: Feedback confirms whether the message was received and understood correctly. Without feedback, communication is incomplete and one-way. It helps the sender correct any misunderstandings.

Q8. Name any four barriers to effective communication. 2 Marks
Model Answer:
  1. Physical Barrier: Noise or distance (e.g., loud surroundings)
  2. Language Barrier: Use of unknown words or different language
  3. Psychological Barrier: Fear, stress, or prejudice
  4. Emotional Barrier: Strong emotions like anger or sadness
Q9. What is active listening? List any two good listening habits. 2 Marks
Model Answer:

Active Listening means giving full attention to the speaker, focusing on what is being said, and responding thoughtfully. It is conscious and deliberate, unlike passive hearing.

Two Good Listening Habits:

  1. Maintain eye contact and avoid distractions
  2. Do not interrupt the speaker; wait for them to finish before responding

Q10. Explain the communication cycle with a neat labelled diagram. 3 Marks

The Communication Cycle is the step-by-step process by which a message travels from sender to receiver and back.

Sender ➜ Idea ➜ Encoding ➜ Channel/Medium ➜ Receiver ➜ Decoding ➜ Feedback ↩

  • Encoding: Converts idea into language/signals

Q11. Explain three types of communication with examples. 3 Marks
Model Answer:

  3. Visual Communication: Conveys information through images, charts, signs. Example: Traffic signals, infographics.

Section D – Long Answer Questions 5 marks each

Q12. What is communication? Explain its importance and any five factors that affect communication. 5 Marks
Model Answer:

Definition: Communication is the process of exchanging information, ideas, feelings or knowledge between two or more persons using a common system of signs, symbols or behaviour.

Importance:

  • Builds and maintains relationships
  • Facilitates learning and knowledge sharing
  • Improves workplace efficiency and teamwork
  • Reduces misunderstandings and conflicts

Five Factors Affecting Communication:

  1. Visual Perception: How we see and interpret the world differs, leading to different understanding of the same message.
  2. Language: Use of complex vocabulary or different language makes communication difficult.
  3. Past Experience: Prior experiences can positively or negatively affect interpretation of a message.
  4. Prejudices: Pre-formed opinions about the sender may cause biased interpretation.
  5. Emotions: Strong feelings (anger, grief) of either sender or receiver can distort communication.
