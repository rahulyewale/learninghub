1. What is Communication?

Communication is the process of exchanging information, ideas, thoughts, feelings or messages between two or more people. The word communication comes from the Latin word communicare meaning "to share".

Importance of Communication

  • Builds and maintains relationships
  • Helps share information and knowledge
  • Reduces misunderstandings and conflicts
  • Improves teamwork and collaboration
  • Essential in every professional field — especially IT-ITeS
Remember: Communication involves at least TWO parties — a sender (who sends the message) and a receiver (who receives and understands it).

4. Non-Verbal Communication

Non-Verbal Communication conveys messages without using words. Studies show that more than 55% of communication is non-verbal.

TypeDescriptionExample
Facial ExpressionsExpressions of face show emotionsSmile = happy; frown = angry
Eye ContactLooking at the listener shows confidence and interestLooking away = disinterest or dishonesty
GesturesMovements of hands, arms, headThumbs up = approval; waving = greeting
PosturePosition and bearing of the bodyStraight posture = confidence; slouching = boredom
Body LanguageOverall physical behaviourArms crossed = defensive; leaning in = interest
ParalanguageVocal cues other than wordsTone, pitch, volume, speed of speech
Personal SpaceDistance maintained while communicating (Proxemics)Standing too close = invasion of space
AppearanceDress, grooming, presentationNeat dress = professionalism
Remember the 7-38-55 rule: In face-to-face communication — 7% words, 38% tone of voice, 55% body language (Albert Mehrabian's research). Examiners sometimes ask about this!

7. Barriers to Communication

Barriers are obstacles that prevent effective communication.

Type of BarrierDescriptionExample
Physical BarriersEnvironmental or distance-related obstaclesNoise, distance, poor signal
Language BarriersDifference in language or vocabularyUsing technical jargon with a layman
Psychological BarriersMental/emotional blocksFear, stress, bias, prejudice
Cultural BarriersDifferent cultural practices and meaningsA gesture meaning "OK" in one country = rude in another
Organisational BarriersToo many levels of hierarchyMessage gets distorted passing through many levels
Perceptual BarriersDifferent interpretation of the same messageSarcasm being taken literally

How to Overcome Barriers

  • Use simple, clear language
  • Choose the right channel for the message
  • Listen actively
  • Seek feedback to confirm understanding
  • Be culturally sensitive
  • Eliminate noise and distractions
Most common barrier in IT-ITeS: Language and jargon barriers — always use simple words with new users or clients.

8. Sample Questions with Answers

Q1. Define communication. 1 Mark▼
Communication is the process of exchanging information, ideas, thoughts or feelings between two or more people through a common system of signs, symbols or language.
Q2. Name the three main methods of communication with one example each. 2 Marks▼
(1) Verbal – conversation or letter. (2) Non-Verbal – smile or eye contact. (3) Visual – traffic sign or chart.
Q3. Differentiate between oral and written communication. 2 Marks▼
Oral communication uses spoken words and gives immediate feedback (e.g., conversation). Written communication uses written words, is permanent, can be referred to later (e.g., email, letter).
Q4. Name any four types of non-verbal communication. 2 Marks▼
Facial expressions, eye contact, gestures, posture, body language, paralanguage, personal space, appearance. (Any four.)
Q5. Differentiate between formal and informal communication. 2 Marks▼
Formal communication uses official channels, professional language and is documented (e.g., business email). Informal communication is casual, personal and usually undocumented (e.g., chatting with friends).
Q6. State any four barriers to effective communication with examples. 4 Marks▼
(1) Physical – noise in the room making it hard to hear. (2) Language – using technical jargon with a non-expert. (3) Psychological – fear or stress preventing clear expression. (4) Cultural – a gesture meaning approval in one culture is offensive in another.
Q7. What is visual communication? Give three examples. 3 Marks▼
Visual communication uses images, symbols and designs to convey information. Examples: traffic signals, bar charts, infographics, posters, maps.
Quick Revision: Communication = exchange of information | Verbal = words (oral + written) | Non-verbal = body language, expressions, gestures | Visual = signs, charts, images | Formal = official | Informal = casual | Barriers: physical, language, psychological, cultural.
