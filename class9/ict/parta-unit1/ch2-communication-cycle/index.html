1. What is the Communication Cycle?

The Communication Cycle describes the complete process of communication from the sender forming an idea to the receiver's feedback reaching back to the sender. It is a continuous, two-way process.

The concept was developed by Claude Shannon and Warren Weaver (1948), who created the first mathematical model of communication.

2. Elements of the Communication Cycle

#ElementMeaningExample
1Sender (Source)The person who originates and sends the messageTeacher explaining a lesson
2Message / IdeaThe information, thought or feeling to be communicatedThe concept of communication cycle
3EncodingConverting the idea into a language/symbols/form the receiver can understandTeacher converts idea into spoken words and diagrams
4Channel / MediumThe path through which the message travelsVoice, telephone, email, letter, TV
5ReceiverThe person who receives the messageStudent listening to the teacher
6DecodingInterpreting and understanding the messageStudent understands the lesson explained
7FeedbackThe receiver's response to the message — confirms understanding or requests clarificationStudent answers a question or asks a doubt
8Noise (Barrier)Any interference that distorts or blocks the messageBackground noise, unclear words, jargon

Encoding vs Decoding: Encoding = SENDER converts idea into message | Decoding = RECEIVER interprets the message. If decoding doesn't match encoding, miscommunication occurs.

4. Types of Feedback

Feedback is the receiver's response. It completes the communication cycle and tells the sender whether the message was understood.

TypeDescriptionExample
Positive FeedbackIndicates message was understood correctly; encourages continuationStudent nods; says "Yes, I understand"
Negative FeedbackIndicates message was NOT understood; prompts correctionStudent says "I don't understand" or looks confused
Verbal FeedbackFeedback given through wordsAsking a question, giving an answer
Non-Verbal FeedbackFeedback through body languageSmiling, nodding, frowning
Immediate FeedbackResponse given right awayReply in a phone call or meeting
Delayed FeedbackResponse given after a time gapReply to an email after a few hours
Feedback is essential: Without feedback, communication is one-way and the sender cannot confirm the message was received and understood.

5. Noise — Interference in Communication

Noise refers to any interference or distortion that prevents the message from being received correctly. It is NOT just sound — it includes any barrier.

Type of NoiseDescriptionExample
Physical / Environmental NoiseExternal disturbances in the environmentLoud music, traffic noise, poor phone signal
Semantic NoiseProblems with meaning — wrong words, jargon, ambiguityUsing technical terms the receiver doesn't know
Psychological NoiseMental distractions or biases of sender/receiverDaydreaming, prejudice, stress
Physiological NoisePhysical conditions affecting communicationIllness, tiredness, hearing problem

6. Characteristics of Effective Communication

Effective communication is known by the 7 C's:

CMeaningExample
ClearThe message is easy to understandUse simple language, avoid jargon
ConciseThe message is brief — no unnecessary wordsSay what you mean in as few words as needed
CorrectFactually accurate; no grammar errorsVerify information before sending
CompleteAll necessary information is includedMention date, time, venue in an event notice
CourteousPolite, respectful toneUse "Please" and "Thank you"; avoid rude language
CoherentLogically ordered and consistentIdeas flow in a logical sequence
ConcreteSpecific facts and details; not vague"Please come at 3 PM" not "come in the afternoon"
Memory trick: Remember 7 C's — Clear, Concise, Correct, Complete, Courteous, Coherent, Concrete.

7. Active Listening

Active Listening means fully concentrating, understanding, responding and remembering what the speaker says — not just hearing the words.

Difference: Hearing vs Listening

HearingListening (Active)
Passive — happens automaticallyActive — requires effort and concentration
Physical process (ear picks up sound)Mental process (brain processes meaning)
No understanding requiredFull understanding of message required

Tips for Active Listening

  • Maintain eye contact with the speaker
  • Avoid interrupting while others are speaking
  • Nod or say "I see" to show engagement
  • Ask relevant questions after the speaker finishes
  • Avoid distractions (phone, daydreaming)
  • Paraphrase — repeat the key idea to confirm understanding

8. Sample Questions with Answers

Q1. Name all elements of the communication cycle. 2 Marks▼
Sender, Message/Idea, Encoding, Channel/Medium, Receiver, Decoding, Feedback, Noise.
Q2. What is encoding? What is decoding? 2 Marks▼
Encoding: The sender converts the idea/message into a language, symbols or form the receiver can understand.
Decoding: The receiver interprets and understands the encoded message.
Q3. What is feedback? Why is it important? 2 Marks▼
Feedback is the receiver's response to the message. It confirms whether the message was understood correctly. Without feedback, communication remains one-directional and incomplete.
Q4. What is noise in communication? Name four types. 2 Marks▼
Noise is any interference that distorts or blocks the message. Types: Physical noise (background sound), Semantic noise (jargon/unclear words), Psychological noise (bias/stress), Physiological noise (illness/tiredness).
Q5. Explain the 7 C's of effective communication. 5 Marks▼
Clear (easy to understand), Concise (brief), Correct (accurate), Complete (all information), Courteous (polite), Coherent (logical), Concrete (specific). These ensure the message is fully and correctly understood by the receiver.
Q6. Differentiate between hearing and active listening. 2 Marks▼
Hearing is passive and automatic — ears pick up sound without necessarily understanding it. Active Listening is deliberate and conscious — the listener focuses, understands, and responds meaningfully to what is said.
Q7. Draw and explain the communication cycle. 5 Marks▼
Diagram: Sender → Encoding → Channel → Receiver → Decoding → Feedback (back to Sender).
Explanation: Sender has an idea, encodes it into words/symbols, sends through a channel (voice, email). Receiver gets message, decodes it, and sends feedback. Noise can disrupt any stage.
Quick Revision: 8 elements: Sender | Message | Encoding | Channel | Receiver | Decoding | Feedback | Noise. Feedback completes the cycle. 7 C's = Clear Concise Correct Complete Courteous Coherent Concrete. Active listening ≠ just hearing.
