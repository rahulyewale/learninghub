🗣️ Chapter 1 — Three Methods of Communication

Communication is the exchange of information, ideas, or feelings between two or more people. There are three main methods:

🗣️ Verbal

Uses words — spoken or written

  • Oral: conversation, phone call, interview, speech, video call
  • Written: letter, email, report, notice, SMS
🤝 Non-Verbal

No words — body language & gestures

  • Facial expressions (smile, frown)
  • Eye contact (confidence)
  • Gestures (thumbs up = OK)
  • Posture (straight = confident)
  • Paralanguage (tone, pitch, volume)
📊 Visual

Uses images, symbols, signs

  • Traffic signs & signals
  • Bar charts, pie charts, graphs
  • Maps (road, weather)
  • Infographics & posters
  • Photographs & videos
7-38-55 Rule (Mehrabian): In face-to-face communication — 7% words, 38% tone of voice, 55% body language. Non-verbal matters most!

Oral vs Written Communication

Oral Communication
  • ✅ Immediate / real-time
  • ✅ Instant feedback possible
  • ✅ Uses tone and expression
  • ⚠️ Not always recorded
  • Example: conversation, phone call
Written Communication
  • ✅ Permanent record
  • ✅ Can be carefully composed
  • ✅ Can be referred to later
  • ⚠️ Delayed feedback
  • Example: letter, email, report
🔄 Chapter 2 — The Communication Cycle

The Communication Cycle shows how a message travels from the sender to the receiver and back as feedback. Developed by Shannon and Weaver (1948).

👤 SENDER Has an idea
→
💭 ENCODING Converts idea to words/symbols
→
📡 CHANNEL Medium of transmission
→
👂 RECEIVER Gets the message
→
🧠 DECODING Interprets meaning
🔇 NOISE / BARRIER Can disrupt any stage — physical, semantic, psychological, physiological
←
💬 FEEDBACK Receiver's response back to sender

Each Element Explained

ElementMeaningReal-World Example
SenderPerson who starts the communicationTeacher explaining a lesson
MessageThe idea / information to be sharedThe concept being taught
EncodingSender converts idea into words/symbolsTeacher forms sentences and diagrams
ChannelThe medium/path the message travelsVoice, blackboard, WhatsApp
ReceiverPerson who receives the messageStudent listening in class
DecodingReceiver interprets and understandsStudent understands the explanation
FeedbackReceiver's response to senderStudent answers a question or nods
NoiseAny interference that distorts the messageFan noise, jargon, distraction

Types of Noise

🔊 Physical

External sounds or environment

Loud fan, traffic, poor phone signal

📖 Semantic

Wrong words, jargon, ambiguity

Using "CPU" with someone unfamiliar

🧠 Psychological

Mental distractions or biases

Daydreaming, stress, prejudice

🤒 Physiological

Physical conditions

Illness, tiredness, hearing impairment

🚧 Chapter 1 — Barriers to Communication

A barrier is anything that prevents the message from reaching the receiver correctly. There are 6 main types:

🌍 Physical

Environment or distance obstacles

Noise, distance, poor internet

🗣️ Language

Different language or vocabulary

Using IT jargon with a non-techie

😟 Psychological

Mental or emotional blocks

Fear, stress, prejudice, bias

🌏 Cultural

Different cultural practices

A thumbs-up is offensive in some countries

🏢 Organisational

Too many hierarchy levels

Message gets distorted through 5 managers

👁️ Perceptual

Different interpretation of same message

Sarcasm taken literally

How to overcome barriers: Use simple language • Choose the right channel • Ask for feedback • Be culturally sensitive • Eliminate distractions

📖 Chapter 3 — Parts of Speech

There are 8 parts of speech in English. Every word in a sentence belongs to one of these categories.

1. Noun

Names a person, place, thing or idea

Rahul, school, love, Delhi

2. Pronoun

Replaces a noun to avoid repetition

he, she, it, they, we, I, you

3. Verb

Expresses action or state of being

run, write, is, seem, think

4. Adjective

Describes or modifies a noun

tall, red, happy, three, few

5. Adverb

Describes a verb, adjective, or adverb

quickly, very, often, quite, well

6. Preposition

Shows relationship between words

in, on, at, under, beside, through

7. Conjunction

Joins words, phrases or clauses

and, but, or, because, although, so

8. Interjection

Expresses a sudden emotion

Wow!, Ouch!, Hurrah!, Oh no!

💬 Chapter 3 — Kinds of Sentences

1. Declarative Sentence

States a fact or opinion. Ends with a full stop.

"She is a brilliant student."

"The sun rises in the east."

2. Interrogative Sentence

Asks a question. Ends with a question mark.

"Where is the library?"

"Did you complete the assignment?"

3. Imperative Sentence

Gives a command or request. Can end with full stop or exclamation.

"Please open your textbooks."

"Submit the form today."

4. Exclamatory Sentence

Expresses strong emotion. Ends with an exclamation mark.

"What a wonderful performance!"

"How beautiful this place is!"

💪 Chapter 3 — Communication Styles

How we express ourselves matters as much as what we say. There are four main communication styles:

✅ Assertive (IDEAL)

Clear, confident and respectful. Expresses needs while respecting others.

"I disagree with this approach because the data shows otherwise. Could we consider option B?"

❌ Aggressive

Forceful, disrespectful. No regard for others' feelings.

"That's a terrible idea. You clearly don't know what you're talking about."

⚠️ Passive

Avoids expressing opinions. Gives in too easily. Leads to resentment.

"Whatever you say... I guess that's fine." (but it's not)

🔀 Passive-Aggressive

Indirect expression of negativity. Sarcasm or silent treatment.

"Oh sure, great idea." (said sarcastically, meaning the opposite)

Always use Assertive communication in professional environments — it is honest, direct and respectful.
✍️ Chapter 4 — The Writing Process

Good writing goes through 5 stages. Skipping any stage reduces quality.

Stage 1 📋 Pre-Writing Plan, brainstorm, research. Who is your audience?
→
Stage 2 📝 Drafting Write the first version. Focus on ideas, not perfection.
→
Stage 3 🔁 Revising Improve content, structure, clarity. Add/remove ideas.
→
Stage 4 🔍 Editing Fix grammar, spelling, punctuation errors.
→
Stage 5 📤 Publishing Send, print or share the final version.
Key difference: Revising = improving IDEAS and content. Editing = fixing ERRORS in language.

📋 Chapter 4 — Notice Writing Format

A notice is a short official announcement. It covers the 5 W's: Who, What, When, Where, Why.

ST. XAVIER'S HIGH SCHOOL, PANVEL
① Organisation
NOTICE
② Heading "NOTICE"
Date: 21 June 2026
③ Date
Annual Sports Day
④ Title

All students of Classes 6–10 are informed that the Annual Sports Day will be held on 30 June 2026 (Monday) at the school ground from 9:00 AM to 1:00 PM. Students must wear sports kit and report by 8:45 AM. Participation is compulsory.

⑤ Body (5 W's)
[Signature]
Cultural Secretary
⑥ Signature

The 5 W's Checklist

WQuestion answeredExample
WhoWho is the audience?All students of Classes 6–10
WhatWhat is happening?Annual Sports Day
WhenDate and time?30 June, 9 AM–1 PM
WhereVenue/location?School ground
WhyPurpose/reason?Annual sports event
Remember: A notice should be brief. Use CAPITAL LETTERS for the word NOTICE. Always include all 5 W's. End with name and designation.
