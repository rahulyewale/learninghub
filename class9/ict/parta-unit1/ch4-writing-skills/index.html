1. Importance of Writing Skills

Writing Skills are the ability to express thoughts, ideas and information clearly and effectively in written form. In the IT-ITeS industry, writing skills are used daily — for emails, reports, documentation, proposals and client communication.

Qualities of Good Writing

  • Clarity: Ideas are expressed clearly and are easy to understand
  • Conciseness: Says what needs to be said without unnecessary words
  • Accuracy: Facts, grammar and spelling are correct
  • Coherence: Ideas flow logically and connect well
  • Appropriate tone: Matches the audience (formal for professionals, informal for friends)

The Writing Process

  1. Pre-writing: Plan and brainstorm ideas (what to write and how)
  2. Drafting: Write the first version of the document
  3. Revising: Improve content, organisation and clarity
  4. Editing: Fix grammar, spelling, and punctuation errors
  5. Publishing/Sending: Share the final written piece

2. Writing a Paragraph

A Paragraph is a group of sentences about ONE main idea. A well-structured paragraph has three parts.

PartPurposeTip
Topic SentenceStates the main idea of the paragraphUsually the first sentence
Supporting SentencesGive details, examples or reasons to support the topicUsually 3–5 sentences
Concluding SentenceWraps up the paragraph or links to the nextUsually the last sentence; may restate the main idea differently

Paragraph Linking Words (Connectives)

PurposeWords/Phrases
Adding informationfurthermore, in addition, also, moreover
Contrasthowever, on the other hand, yet, but, although
Cause/Effecttherefore, as a result, consequently, hence
Examplesfor example, for instance, such as
Conclusionin conclusion, to summarise, finally
Remember: One paragraph = one idea. Never mix two unrelated topics in one paragraph.

3. Formal Letter Writing

A Formal Letter is written for official purposes — to authorities, companies, banks, schools etc. It uses formal language and a specific format.

Format of a Formal Letter

PartDescription
1. Sender's AddressWriter's address (top-left or top-right)
2. DateDate of writing (below sender's address)
3. Receiver's AddressName, designation and address of recipient
4. SalutationDear Sir / Dear Madam / Dear Mr. [Name]
5. Subject LineBrief title: "Re: Application for Leave"
6. BodyOpening para (purpose) + Main para (details) + Closing para (request/action)
7. ClosingYours sincerely / Yours faithfully
8. SignatureHandwritten signature + printed name

Sample Formal Letter

A-101, Dynamic Enclave
Panvel, Raigarh (MH) – 410206
21 June 2026

The Principal
St. Xavier's High School
Panvel, Maharashtra

Subject: Application for Leave

Dear Sir/Madam,

I am Rahul Yewale, a student of Class 9A (Roll No. 1). I wish to inform you that I will be unable to attend school from 25 to 27 June 2026 due to a family function requiring my presence out of the city.

I request you to kindly grant me leave for three days. I will complete all missed work upon my return.

Yours sincerely,
Rahul Yewale
Class 9A | Roll No. 1
Yours sincerely — when you know the recipient's name | Yours faithfully — when you begin with Dear Sir/Madam (don't know name).

4. Informal Letter Writing

An Informal Letter is written to friends, family or close acquaintances. The language is casual and friendly.

FeatureFormal LetterInformal Letter
AudienceOfficials, companies, institutionsFriends, family, relatives
LanguageFormal, professional, politeCasual, friendly, conversational
SalutationDear Sir/MadamDear Priya / Hi Rohit
ClosingYours sincerely/faithfullyWith love / Your friend / Take care
Subject lineRequiredNot required

5. Writing a Professional Email

Email is the most common written communication in IT-ITeS. A professional email must be clear, concise and properly structured.

Structure of a Professional Email

PartWhat to include
To / CC / BCCRecipient(s) — use CC for information copies, BCC for blind copies
Subject LineShort and specific — e.g., "Meeting Rescheduled to 25 June 2026"
SalutationDear Mr./Ms. [Name] or Dear [First Name] for familiar contacts
Opening LineState purpose: "I am writing to request…" or "Further to our meeting…"
BodyMain content in short, clear paragraphs. One idea per paragraph.
Call to ActionWhat you need the reader to do: "Please confirm by Friday."
ClosingBest regards / Warm regards / Yours sincerely
SignatureFull name, designation, company, phone number
Email don'ts: Never use ALL CAPS (appears as shouting), avoid slang/abbreviations (LOL, BRB) in formal emails, and always proofread before sending.

6. Notice Writing

A Notice is a short official announcement informing people about an event, activity or important information. It is usually displayed on a notice board.

Format of a Notice

  1. Organisation Name / School Name at the top
  2. NOTICE — heading, in capital letters, centred
  3. Date — on the right or below heading
  4. Heading / Title — what the notice is about
  5. Body — who, what, when, where, why (5W's)
  6. Signature — Name and designation of the issuing authority

Sample Notice

ST. XAVIER'S HIGH SCHOOL, PANVEL

NOTICE
Date: 21 June 2026

Annual Sports Day

This is to inform all students that the Annual Sports Day will be held on 30 June 2026 (Monday) at the school ground from 9:00 AM to 1:00 PM. All students from Classes 6 to 10 must participate. Sports kits must be worn. Students must report by 8:45 AM.

Signed,
Cultural Secretary
St. Xavier's High School
Notice is NOT an essay — it should be short, clear and informative. Always include all 5 W's: Who, What, When, Where, Why.

7. Report Writing (Introduction)

A Report is a structured document presenting information and findings on a particular subject. Reports are common in IT-ITeS for project updates, bug reports, analysis etc.

Types of Reports

TypePurposeExample
Formal ReportDetailed, structured; for official submissionAnnual report, project report
Informal ReportShort memo or note to a supervisorProgress update email
Progress ReportUpdates on work doneWeekly status report
Incident ReportRecords an event that occurredAccident or security incident report

Basic Structure of a Formal Report

  1. Title / Heading
  2. Introduction (background and purpose)
  3. Findings / Body (main content — facts, data)
  4. Conclusion / Summary
  5. Recommendations (if required)
  6. Signature / Date

8. Sample Questions with Answers

Q1. What are the five stages of the writing process? 2 Marks▼
Pre-writing (planning), Drafting (first version), Revising (improving content), Editing (fixing errors), Publishing/Sending (sharing the final piece).
Q2. What are the three parts of a well-structured paragraph? 2 Marks▼
Topic Sentence (states main idea), Supporting Sentences (details/examples), Concluding Sentence (wraps up the idea).
Q3. Write the format of a formal letter. 4 Marks▼
Sender's Address → Date → Receiver's Address → Salutation (Dear Sir/Madam) → Subject Line → Body (opening, main, closing paragraphs) → Closing (Yours sincerely/faithfully) → Signature and Name.
Q4. What is the difference between "Yours sincerely" and "Yours faithfully"? 1 Mark▼
"Yours sincerely" is used when the recipient's name is known (Dear Mr. Sharma). "Yours faithfully" is used when the name is not known (Dear Sir/Madam).
Q5. Write a notice (about 60 words) for a school Annual Day. 5 Marks▼
Model Answer:
SCHOOL NAME | NOTICE | Date: __
Annual Day Celebration
All students are informed that the Annual Day will be celebrated on [Date] at [Venue] from [Time]. All classes must attend in school uniform. Cultural programme participants should report 30 minutes early. Parents are cordially invited.
– Cultural Secretary
Q6. Differentiate between formal and informal letters. 2 Marks▼
Formal letters are written to officials/companies using professional language and a specific format (subject line, Yours sincerely). Informal letters are written to friends/family using casual, friendly language without strict format.
Q7. What are the 5 W's of a notice? Why are they important? 2 Marks▼
5 W's = Who (audience), What (event/purpose), When (date and time), Where (venue/location), Why (reason). They ensure the notice gives all necessary information clearly so readers know what to do.
Quick Revision: Writing process = Pre-write → Draft → Revise → Edit → Publish | Paragraph = Topic + Support + Conclusion | Formal letter = 8 parts | Notice = Name+NOTICE+Date+Title+Body+Signature | 5 W's = Who What When Where Why | Yours sincerely = known name | Yours faithfully = Dear Sir/Madam.
