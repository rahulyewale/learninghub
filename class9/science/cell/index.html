1. Cell – The Basic Unit of Life

The cell is the structural and functional unit of life. All living organisms are made of cells.

Cells may be unicellular (one cell) or multicellular (many cells).

Cell Theory (basic idea)

  • All living organisms are made of cells.
  • The cell is the basic unit of structure and function.
  • New cells arise from pre-existing cells.
Memory tip: Think of the cell as a tiny factory that makes, stores, and transports materials.

Cell Membrane

The cell membrane is selectively permeable, meaning it allows only certain substances to pass through.

Exam point: Both plant and animal cells have cell membrane, cytoplasm and nucleus.

4. Osmosis and Diffusion

Diffusion is the movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration.

Osmosis is the movement of water through a selectively permeable membrane from higher water concentration to lower water concentration.

Types of Osmotic Solutions

TypeMeaningEffect on cell
HypotonicOutside solution has more waterWater enters cell; cell swells
HypertonicOutside solution has less waterWater leaves cell; cell shrinks
IsotonicSame water concentration inside and outsideNo net movement of water
Mnemonic: Hyper = higher solute outside, Hypo = lower solute outside, Iso = identical concentrations.

Plasmolysis

When a plant cell loses water in a hypertonic solution, the cell membrane shrinks away from the cell wall. This is called plasmolysis.

6. Sample Questions with Answers

Q1. What is a cell? 1 Mark
Answer: A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life.
Q2. Name the control centre of the cell. 1 Mark
Answer: The nucleus is the control centre of the cell.
Q3. Differentiate between plant cell and animal cell. 2 Marks
Answer: Plant cells have a cell wall, plastids and a large central vacuole, while animal cells do not have a cell wall or plastids and usually have smaller vacuoles.
Q4. What is osmosis? 2 Marks
Answer: Osmosis is the movement of water through a selectively permeable membrane from a region of higher water concentration to lower water concentration.
Q5. What is plasmolysis? 2 Marks
Answer: Plasmolysis is the shrinking of the cell membrane away from the cell wall when a plant cell loses water in a hypertonic solution.
Q6. Why is the cell membrane called selectively permeable? 2 Marks
Answer: It allows only certain substances to enter or leave the cell while restricting others.
Q7. State the functions of any three cell organelles. 3 Marks
Answer: Mitochondria release energy, ribosomes synthesize proteins, and Golgi apparatus packages and secretes materials.
Q8. Why are lysosomes called suicide bags? 3 Marks
Answer: Lysosomes contain digestive enzymes. If they burst, they can digest the cell's own components, so they are called suicide bags.
Q9. Explain the role of the vacuole in plant cells. 2 Marks
Answer: Vacuoles store water, food and wastes. In plant cells, the large central vacuole also helps maintain cell shape and turgidity.
Q10. Give two examples to show the importance of cells. 2 Marks
Answer: Cells help in growth of the body and also repair damaged tissues by cell division.

7. Quick Revision Tips

  • Mitochondria produce energy
  • Ribosomes make proteins
  • Cell wall is only in plants
  • Osmosis = water movement through membrane
  • Hypertonic solution makes plant cell shrink
