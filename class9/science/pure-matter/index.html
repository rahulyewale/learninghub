1. What is Matter?

Matter is anything that has mass and occupies space.

NCERT idea: Matter can exist as a pure substance or a mixture.
Important: In daily life many things look pure, but may actually be mixtures.

Types of Pure Substance

TypeMeaningExamples
ElementMade of only one kind of atomIron, oxygen, gold
CompoundTwo or more elements chemically combined in a fixed ratioWater (H2O), salt (NaCl)
Remember: A compound has a fixed composition, but a mixture does not.

2. Solutions

A solution is a homogeneous mixture of two or more substances.

TermMeaning
SolventComponent present in larger amount; does the dissolving
SoluteSubstance that dissolves in the solvent

Examples

  • Salt in water
  • Sugar in water
  • Air (a mixture of gases)

Characteristics of a True Solution

  • Homogeneous
  • Particle size is very small
  • Particles are not visible to the naked eye
  • Does not scatter light
  • Cannot be separated by filtration
Type of solutionExampleSoluteSolvent
Solid in liquidSalt in waterSaltWater
Liquid in liquidAlcohol in waterAlcoholWater
Gas in liquidCarbonated waterCO2Water
Gas in gasAirOxygen, CO2, etc.Nitrogen

Concentration of a Solution

Concentration tells us how much solute is present in a given amount of solvent or solution.

Concentration = Amount of solute / Amount of solution

Tip: If two drinks have the same volume, the one with more solute is more concentrated.

3. Types of Mixtures

MixtureNatureExampleCan you see components?
HomogeneousUniform throughoutSalt solutionNo
HeterogeneousNot uniform throughoutSand in waterYes / Sometimes

Suspension

  • Heterogeneous mixture
  • Particles are larger and visible
  • Particles settle on standing
  • Can be separated by filtration

Colloids

A colloid looks homogeneous to the naked eye but is actually heterogeneous.

  • Particle size is between solution and suspension
  • Does not settle on standing
  • Shows Tyndall effect (scattering of light)

This is done by centrifugation or churning. Lighter fat particles separate from denser liquid.

7. Sample Questions with Answers

Q1. Define matter and mention two characteristics. 1 Mark
Answer: Matter is anything that has mass and occupies space. Two characteristics are: it has mass and it occupies space.
Q2. Differentiate between a pure substance and a mixture. 2 Marks
Answer: A pure substance has fixed composition and definite properties, while a mixture has variable composition and its components can be separated by physical methods.
Q3. What is a solution? Name the solute and solvent in salt water. 2 Marks
Answer: A solution is a homogeneous mixture. In salt water, salt is the solute and water is the solvent.
Q4. Why is air called a mixture? 2 Marks
Answer: Air contains different gases like nitrogen, oxygen, carbon dioxide, water vapour, etc. Their composition can vary, and they are not chemically combined, so air is a mixture.
Q5. Explain the Tyndall effect with one example. 2 Marks
Answer: The Tyndall effect is the scattering of light by colloidal particles. Example: a beam of light is visible in fog or smoke.
Q6. Why can a suspension be separated by filtration but a colloid cannot? 3 Marks
Answer: Suspension particles are larger and do not pass through filter paper, so they can be filtered. Colloid particles are too small to be trapped by ordinary filter paper, so they pass through.
Q7. State any three methods used to separate a mixture and give one example for each. 3 Marks
Answer: Filtration—sand and water; evaporation—salt from sea water; separating funnel—oil and water.
Q8. A student wants to obtain pure water from salt solution. Which method should be used? Why? 3 Marks
Answer: Distillation should be used because it removes the dissolved salt and then condenses the vapour to give pure water.
Q9. Write two differences between true solution and suspension. 3 Marks
Answer: In a true solution, particles are not visible and do not settle; in a suspension, particles are visible and settle on standing. A true solution cannot be separated by filtration, but a suspension can be.
Q10. What are colloids? Give two examples. 2 Marks
Answer: Colloids are mixtures with particle size between true solutions and suspensions; they appear homogeneous but are actually heterogeneous. Examples: milk and fog.

8. Quick Revision Tips

  • Pure substance = fixed composition
  • Solution = homogeneous mixture
  • Solvent is in larger amount; solute dissolves in it
  • Colloid shows Tyndall effect; suspension settles down
  • Choose separation method by property difference
  • Oil + water → separating funnel; salt + water → evaporation/distillation
  • Milk is a colloid, not a true solution
